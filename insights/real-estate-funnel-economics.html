Real estate growth / Field guide

A cheap lead is only the first number

Follow the cohort from spend to qualified enquiry to booking.

Use one cohort

Pick leads generated in one stated period and follow those same leads through the funnel. Separate bookings from older enquiries. Report media spend separately from agency fees, creative costs and sales costs. Otherwise two cost-per-booking figures can look comparable while measuring different things.

Calculate the useful ratios

Media CPL = media spend ÷ generated leads. Cost per qualified lead = media spend ÷ qualified leads. Lead-to-booking rate = confirmed bookings ÷ generated leads. Media cost per booking = media spend ÷ confirmed bookings. Show the underlying counts and use “not available” when a denominator or outcome is missing.

Read the QDnCo example carefully

Management reports one India case with ₹6 lakh media spend, 1,700+ leads and 51 bookings. Using 1,700 as the calculation base gives about ₹353 per lead, ₹11,765 media spend per booking and 3% lead-to-booking conversion. The plus sign means the exact CPL and rate require the exact lead count. These are reported historical outcomes, not a promise for the next project.

Do not turn inventory into agency return

Property value attached to bookings is not cash collected, developer profit or agency revenue. Pipeline influence is a separate measure again. Include cancellations and collection status before making a realized-sales claim. QDnCo’s separate 3.5–4% paying-lead statement refers to other reported cases, not the 51-booking example.

Compare without mixing markets

Keep INR and AED cohorts separate. In Dubai, track reservation, later payment and cancellation as distinct outcomes where relevant to the project. In India, distinguish enquiry, visit, booking and collection. Ask the sales team to define the evidence for each stage before presenting a return figure.
