Give the buyer something worth keeping

Build a project comparison sheet that earns the next conversation.

Choose a real buying decision

A useful resource helps someone choose between alternatives. A brochure repeats project features; a comparison sheet explains which constraints change the choice. Pick one decision: location versus budget, ready possession versus a future delivery plan, or two unit options within the same project.

Use a transparent comparison

Create rows for current quoted price, unit size basis, payment schedule, delivery status, recurring charges, travel assumptions and source date. Leave unknowns visible. Do not combine carpet area and saleable area into an apparent like-for-like price comparison. Record the source and date for every changing number.

India edition

For a Bangalore or Chennai comparison, start with the buyer’s work location, budget and possession needs. Link to the relevant official project disclosures and current written offer. Keep estimated travel time separate from a guarantee. Have the project team verify pricing, availability and permitted marketing claims before distribution.

Dubai edition

For an overseas buyer, explain the questions to ask about the payment schedule, handover status, service charges and ongoing ownership costs. Use current project documents and authoritative records when available. Do not invent rental yields, future appreciation or guaranteed returns to make the sheet more persuasive.

Build the next step into the resource

End with a specific invitation: “Want the comparison for your budget and preferred location?” Ask only for details needed to tailor that comparison. For QDnCo’s developer and channel-partner audience, the invitation is different: “Let’s design a buyer resource for your project.” Keep the two funnels separate so consumer enquiries do not enter the agency sales pipeline.
