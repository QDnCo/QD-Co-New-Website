Real estate growth / Field guide

More enquiries. Who calls them back?

A practical capacity plan for real estate sales teams.

Calculate your actual room

Start with the staffed minutes available after meetings, visits and existing-client work. Divide by the observed average minutes needed for a new enquiry’s first handling cycle. Reserve capacity for follow-ups; a team that uses every minute on new leads builds tomorrow’s backlog.

Make ownership visible

Every new enquiry needs one owner, a due time and a recorded outcome. Use a backup owner for absences. Separate “attempted”, “connected” and “qualified”; a dial attempt is not a conversation. Review ageing by campaign and project so one busy launch does not conceal an unattended queue.

A missed-call message

Example, adapt to the actual enquiry: “Hi [name], this is [agent] from [business]. You asked about [project]. I tried calling to help with [requested detail]. Would [time A] or [time B] suit you? If you prefer, I can send the details here.” Identify yourself and respect the person’s contact preference. Do not send unsolicited voice notes or repeated follow-ups after an opt-out.

Use voice when it helps

A short, natural voice note can answer a specific question after the buyer welcomes that format. Keep a text alternative for someone at work or in a different language. Do not assume voice notes outperform calls for every audience; compare connected conversations and agreed next steps.

Plan around geography

Bangalore and Chennai teams need visit availability as well as calling capacity. Dubai teams serving overseas buyers need timezone-aware ownership. Reduce or redistribute acquisition only when staffing and backlog evidence justify it; do not turn a generic daily cap into a rule for every project.
